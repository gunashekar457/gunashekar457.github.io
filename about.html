The Game I Wasn't Supposed to Play

My name is B. Gunasekhar. I’m a Gameplay Programmer from India, but my story with games started long before I ever imagined I could make a career out of them.

Like many Asian kids, I grew up hearing the usual warnings:

  • “Don’t waste your time playing games.”
  • “Games won’t get you anywhere.”
  • “Study first. Games later.”

So, naturally… I became very good at finding ways to play them.

When I was around seven, I would literally climb over the walls of my house and sneak into the neighboring home just to play games on a floppy-disk console.

A few years later, I discovered internet cafés. That was where I met games like IGI and GTA: San Andreas. Those cafés became little worlds where I could explore places, take on challenges, and experience stories far beyond my own surroundings.

By fourteen, I had my own laptop. It was supposed to be for studying. Instead, it became my gateway to games like Criminal Case and Dragon City. I would play when I was supposed to be studying, and whenever I heard my parents coming into the room, I would quickly switch tabs and pretend I was doing something productive.

It worked sometimes. It definitely didn't work every time.

But somewhere between trying to hide the games and wondering how those games were actually made, something changed. I stopped wanting to just play them.

I wanted to understand them.

The idea of becoming a game developer finally clicked for me during my intermediate years. What started as curiosity slowly became a direction. I began learning programming, game engines, gameplay systems, and everything I could about how games actually work.

And now, I’m here. Still learning. Still experimenting. Still trying to build my first great game.

I haven't shipped a game that changes the world. I haven't built the next GTA.

Not yet.

But the kid who once had to sneak around just to play games can now sit down at his computer and try to create one. And for me, that's already pretty incredible.

This is only the beginning.
